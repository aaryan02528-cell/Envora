## LCD/I²C Integration

### Hardware Wiring
The 16×2 character LCD with PCF8574 I²C backpack was connected to the ESP32-C3:
- **SDA** $\rightarrow$ **GPIO 4**
- **SCL** $\rightarrow$ **GPIO 5**
- **VCC** $\rightarrow$ **5V**
- **GND** $\rightarrow$ **GND**

### Bus Discovery & Display Setup
1. **I²C Scanner Verification**: An I²C bus scanner was flashed with SDA assigned to GPIO 4 and SCL to GPIO 5. The scanner successfully identified an active peripheral at address `0x27`.
2. **Contrast Adjustment**: Initial power-on showed backlight and character blocks without clear text output. Turning the contrast potentiometer on the LCD backpack resolved the issue and rendered character output visible.
3. **Text Rendering**: Initialized LiquidCrystal_I2C driver and verified string rendering and cursor control on both display rows.

---

## Combined Sensor + Display Test

Following independent LCD display validation, the DHT11 sensor and LCD display were integrated into a unified hardware prototype on the breadboard:

$$\text{ESP32-C3} \longrightarrow \text{DHT11 (GPIO 3)} \longrightarrow \text{LCD I²C (GPIO 4/5, 0x27)}$$

Dedicated validation firmware [`dht11_lcd_test.ino`](file:///C:/Users/aarya/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/Envora/firmware/src/dht11_lcd_test/dht11_lcd_test.ino) was flashed. The MCU successfully read ambient telemetry from the DHT11 sensor every 3 seconds, output the data over UART Serial Monitor, and updated both lines of the 16×2 LCD with live temperature (°C) and humidity (%) metrics.

## Engineering Learning

This validation reinforced Envora's **incremental fault-isolation engineering methodology**:

$$\text{ESP32-C3} \longrightarrow \text{DHT11} \longrightarrow \text{I²C Scanner} \longrightarrow \text{LCD} \longrightarrow \text{DHT11 + LCD Combined Test}$$

By confirming bus connectivity with an I²C scanner before deploying display code, and by tuning hardware contrast before assuming driver failures, each subsystem was validated independently before full combined verification.
